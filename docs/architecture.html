A copilot for live calls

It listens to an online call on your Mac, notices when someone asks you a question, and shows a short answer taken only from the documents you chose, with the source of every line.

Status: working prototype. Setup screen, audio capture, local transcription, question check, answers and the floating window work and are measured.

Where each part runs

Everything runs on your Mac except one step: the question and your documents go to Claude to write the answer. The call app is not touched, and the copilot does not join the meeting.

  • audio
  • text inside the copilot
  • the only hop off the Mac
  • What you install: a small Swift helper that captures audio, a Python app, and the Whisper speech model (about 1.6 GB, downloaded once). No audio driver, no change to the call app.
  • macOS permission: on first run, "Screen & System Audio Recording". The purple indicator in the menu bar is visible only to you.
  • Who speaks: two separate streams, so only the other side's questions trigger an answer.
  • Language: the speech model is multilingual, and the answer comes back in the language of the question.

From the end of a question to the answer

The copilot cuts the audio at short pauses. When a sentence ends, it transcribes it on the Mac, checks whether it is a question for you, and streams the answer into the window. The times below were measured on a MacBook Pro with Apple Silicon.

What the others see

In the call

  • No "Recording" badge: the call app's recording is not used.
  • No extra participant: the copilot does not join the meeting.
  • Your voice and audio work as always.

If you share your screen

  • The copilot window is excluded from screen sharing: you see it, they do not.
  • It can also sit on a second monitor.

Rules of use

Privacy and consent

  • Nothing is saved by default: transcript and answers disappear on exit.
  • Tell the others at the start that an assistant takes notes. In the EU and in Switzerland, transcribing someone's voice without telling them is a legal risk.
  • Audio stays on the Mac; Claude receives only the text of the question and your documents.

When not to use it

  • In exams or assessed presentations.
  • When a fact is not in the documents: the window says "Not in the material" instead of guessing.

Choice of models and cost

question detection

Claude Haiku 4.5: the fastest and cheapest. It reads one line at a time, a fraction of a cent per check.

answers

Claude Sonnet 5.5 at low effort, thinking off. In testing it kept the 3-line format, cited sources and invented nothing; Claude Haiku 4.5 broke the format and once invented figures.

cost, 84k-token documents

About $0.43 to cache the documents for an hour, then about $0.024 per question: under $1 for an hour with 20 questions.
